My Research

Lithium-6 in an extremely Li-rich, very metal-poor giant

Master's thesis · Uppsala University · 2026 – present

Standard Big Bang nucleosynthesis predicts virtually no 6Li in old, very metal-poor stars, yet Sitnova et al. (2023) reported a 6Li detection in a Li-rich, very metal-poor giant using 1D non-LTE models. I am reanalysing this star with self-consistent 3D non-LTE spectral synthesis (Stagger model atmospheres and the Balder radiative-transfer code), which capture the convective line asymmetries that can mimic the 6Li signature.

The 6Li/7Li ratio is fitted to high-resolution Subaru/HDS spectra with Bayesian MCMC, and the 3D calculations run on the Dardel supercomputer (NAISS). The project started as a course project supervised by Karin Lind and Ella Xi Wang and is now being extended in my Master's thesis.

Course project report available on request.

Neutrinos from neutrino-cooled stars

Research project · Uppsala University · Sep – Dec 2024

For a 20 solar-mass star in its final, neutrino-cooled stage, I computed the time-dependent rates of pair-annihilation neutrinos of all flavours, including MSW oscillation effects, in the JUNO, KamLAND and Super-Kamiokande-Gd detectors. In the best case, a star at 0.2 kpc could be detected up to six months before core collapse, while beyond 10 kpc detection appears impossible. Supervised by Andreas Korn and Erin O'Sullivan.

Variable-range hopping in granular metals

Bachelor's thesis · University of Murcia · 2024

Monte Carlo simulations (Metropolis–Hastings, written in Fortran 90) of electron transport by variable-range hopping in a square lattice of metallic grains with random energies. For three disorder ranges, the conductivity follows an activated (exponential) temperature dependence at weak disorder and a softer law at strong disorder. Supervised by Miguel Ortuño Ortín and Andrés Manuel Somoza Gimeno.
